# Shadowfell: quest research and original quest catalogue

Research date: 31 October 2026. Scope: research and design only; no game files changed.

The most useful lesson across these five games is that a familiar action becomes memorable when its purpose, rules, or consequences change. A delivery can test route planning; a conversation can uncover a mistaken accusation; a fight can protect a process rather than fill a counter. Shadowfell can achieve this with its existing small settlements and named residents before adding new continents.

## How to read this report

The **verified mechanic** column summarizes source material. **Placement, complexity, narrative purpose, and adaptation** are my design interpretations, not statements by the original developers. Early/mid/late are normalized design placements, not equivalent character levels between games. Exact source requirements are included only where explicitly documented. Historical walkthroughs establish examples, not guaranteed current rewards or eligibility. RuneScape entries below refer to RuneScape rather than silently mixing in Old School RuneScape.

Complexity means the number of interacting rules and decisions, not enemy strength: **simple** = one rule or short local loop; **moderate** = several related steps or one meaningful decision; **advanced** = a branching route, multiple interdependent clues, or a sustained bespoke sequence. A level 1 quest can be intellectually demanding. Proposed Shadowfell levels are design targets, not existing content claims.

LOTRO Wiki returned a verification barrier. LOTRO examples therefore use official release notes where useful and named walkthroughs for mechanics. Lost Ark Codex's search-indexed entry was readable while its direct page returned a captcha; that limited evidence is identified below. Conflicting eligibility claims for Broken Home are deliberately not resolved by guessing.

## Original Shadowfell quests: 26 proposals

Everything in this section is original design, not a claim about the games above or existing Shadowfell content. Existing named NPCs are reused as potential givers; new people, objects, states, and interactions are explicitly proposals. All entries are optional and independent of the main story unless the user later chooses otherwise. Entering a dangerous region can require appropriate combat readiness, but none of these quests should unlock or block the established boar → lanterns → Bellkeeper → erased names → crypt-keeper story.

The current-zone proposals avoid replaying the existing boundary judgment, Briarjaw/pigs, apples-for-reeds message, lost brooch pouch, perch supper, ditchbell collection, wolf clearing, and Widow's Mirror remembrance tale.

## Progression and anti-repetition guide

1. **Give each zone a dominant activity and a counterpoint.** Thornford: social competence plus a small physical repair. Orchard: practical ecology plus processing. Pond: gentle technique plus spatial observation. Briarwood: tracks and cautious movement. Road: protected objectives and signals. Hollow: diagnosis and ambiguity. Graveyard/crypt: pressure and interlocking space. Do not make every spooky location secretly part of the same conspiracy.
2. **Rotate verbs, not just targets.** Across any five accepted quests, aim for at least four leading verbs such as inspect, arrange, track, negotiate, repair, protect, or navigate. Do not offer three simultaneous collect-and-return jobs merely because their materials differ.
3. **Teach one rule, then combine two.** Level 1 introduces visible feedback and reversible errors. Level 2 adds a second clue or route choice. Levels 3–4 combine observation with danger or a civic consequence. Level 5 offers a short synthesis with a checkpoint. Future tiers can deepen interactions without increasing every counter.
4. **Keep local stories complete.** A side quest should solve the concern that started it. An optional future hook may follow, but do not turn every repaired fence, missing traveler, or household noise into evidence for the main villain.
5. **Let evidence alter the task.** Prefer “find out why the mixture spoiled, then fix that cause” to “collect ingredients, then collect more ingredients.” Show the player the change of understanding in the journal.
6. **Make branches change one observable thing.** A person moves, a stall gains stock, a roof gets a patch, or a dialogue line changes. Equalize core XP so a humane or non-combat outcome is not secretly punished. Keep outcomes bounded to avoid an unmaintainable continent-wide branching story.
7. **Avoid MMO friction.** No daily requirement for story closure, no random quest-item drop needed for a tiny local errand, no multi-hour event schedule, and no mandatory party for a village story. Use optional repeatable challenges only after the narrative has concluded.
8. **Budget duration deliberately.** Suggested targets: simple 3–6 minutes; moderate 7–12; advanced 12–20, excluding deliberate exploration. These are design targets to test, not measured implementation estimates. Make three chained favors the normal maximum in the starter region.
9. **Use fail-forward teaching.** Reset the current puzzle or encounter, not the entire quest. Mark understood clues, support visual equivalents of sound, and offer a contextual hint after a failed attempt. Timers should create an optional mastery bonus unless time pressure is the explicit appeal.
10. **Reward the world as well as the inventory.** A fixed gate, working footrail, satisfied guest, unlocked shortcut, or new repair service makes a small reward memorable. Avoid adding large stat inflation to every quiet errand.

## Suggested first production batch

Build S01, S02, S07, S09, S11, S14, and S17 first. Together they cover profession diagnosis, social arrangement, ecology, fishing technique, evidence-based tracking, a combat alternative, and an apothecary puzzle across the existing map. They avoid new continent work and reduce repetition around the existing boar/wolf/undead progression. S21 and S23–S26 should wait until the needed spatial-puzzle and consequence systems are proven.

For every approved quest, specify: giver and exact trigger; zone and recommended level; player verbs; required clues; success and recoverable-failure states; alternative routes; reward; visible aftermath; journal text; save/load behavior; and the existing story states it must not alter. This is the handoff boundary between these research seeds and an implementation-ready design.
